AI GOVERNANCE / PERSPECTIVE

The AI control plane: governance that keeps up with deployment.

AI adoption is moving faster than most governance processes were designed to operate. The answer is not simply more policy. Organizations need an AI control plane: a practical operating model connecting use cases, risk classification, approvals, model providers, data, controls and evidence.

What changes

  • Move from annual policy review to live governance decisions
  • Classify AI by business impact and risk rather than technology label
  • Make model and use-case inventory operational
  • Connect security, privacy, legal and business accountability

What leaders should ask

  • Which AI decisions require escalation?
  • Who can approve high-impact use cases?
  • Can we prove which controls were applied?
  • How do third-party models change our risk?
CYBERSECURITY / EXECUTIVE BRIEF

Cyber resilience after prevention.

Prevention remains essential, but no credible security strategy can assume every control will hold. Resilience begins by defining the critical services the business must continue, understanding their technology and third-party dependencies, and designing recovery decisions before an incident.

Resilience means

  • Critical service mapping
  • Credible disruption scenarios
  • Identity and privileged access recovery
  • Third-party dependency visibility
  • Executive crisis decision readiness

Measure differently

  • Time to contain material exposure
  • Time to recover critical services
  • Quality of recovery evidence
  • Decision speed under uncertainty
  • Remediation of recurring failure modes
IT AUDIT / POINT OF VIEW

From annual audit to continuous assurance.

Technology changes continuously while assurance is often periodic. Better data, control telemetry and workflow evidence can make audit more current—but continuous assurance only works when independence, ownership and exception logic remain clear.

Where to start

  • Identify controls with reliable system evidence
  • Define meaningful risk thresholds
  • Separate management monitoring from independent assurance
  • Automate evidence collection before automating conclusions

Avoid

  • Dashboard theater without control accountability
  • Excessive metrics with no decision owner
  • False assurance from incomplete telemetry
  • Automation that obscures exceptions
LEADERSHIP / BOARD REPORTING

Board reporting should be a decision system, not a status pack.

The strongest cyber and technology risk reporting does not overwhelm leaders with vulnerabilities, controls or project updates. It connects business exposure, risk appetite, evidence, action ownership and the decisions leadership actually needs to make.

Board-level structure

  • Business services and material exposure
  • Risk appetite and threshold breaches
  • Control health and evidence confidence
  • Critical remediation and decision blockers

Questions to answer

  • What changed since the last meeting?
  • What could materially affect the business?
  • What decision is required now?
  • What evidence supports the conclusion?
